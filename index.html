About me

I am a particle physicst currently working as a senior research fellow at ETH Zurich and CERN. When I'm not buried in a book on the history of science or the philosophy of science, I like to use Machine Learning to try to improve the way we collect and analyze data at the Large Hadron Collider. My favorite topic is real-time Machine Learning, how we can use efficient ML to help us process enormous data rates (63 Tb/s) at LHC, within a latency of 12 microseconds (and often sub 100 ns!). I also really like to think about, and develop, cool new methods for ML-based anomaly detection as a way of discovering New Physics (spoiler alert: haven't found anything yet!). When I am not writing code or watching YouTube videos on Machine Learning, you can find me hunting for the nearest hill to go trail running. Working at a university, one of the things I value the most is to supervise students on projects, as well as give lectures and schools on ML and partice physics. Don't be afraid to send me an e-mail (see below) if you have any questions or you would like to work together on a fun project!
